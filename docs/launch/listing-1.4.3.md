## 0. THE HONEST ANSWER ABOUT ASO, FIRST

**Apple does not search the description.** The fields Apple's own guidance
names as searchable are the **app name**, the **subtitle** and the **keyword
field** — plus, less obviously, the **in-app purchase display names**. The long
description is read by people and not by the search index. (This is the
opposite of Google Play, which does index its description, and it is where
most ASO advice on the internet goes wrong for iOS.)

So a description rewrite cannot move a ranking directly. What it moves is
**conversion** — the share of people who, having landed on the page, tap Get —
and conversion is itself an input to ranking, so it helps by the long way
round. It is worth doing, and it is done below.

**But the ranking headroom is in three other places**, and they are listed in
order of how much they are worth against how much they cost:

| | Field | State | Worth doing? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Keywords** | 93 of 100 characters used; the word **widget** appears in no indexed field at all, and `widget,` is exactly 7 characters | **YES — free, zero risk** |
| 2 | **Screenshots** | Not one of the ten shows a widget; position 3 is a viewfinder | **YES — see §5** |
| 3 | **Subtitle** | 27 of 30 characters, unchanged since launch, and most of them are not search terms | **Her call — see §3** |
| 4 | App name | Changed one week ago | **No. Leave it.** |
| 5 | Subscription display names | Indexed, and currently "Premium Monthly" / "Premium Annual" | Minor. See §7. |

### What changed from what is live, and why each one

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **The opening two lines are rebuilt** | Apple cuts the description at about three lines. The old first line was a metaphor ("the dashboard your music deserves"); this one shows the product in the only space most readers will ever see. | **"BUILT FOR APPLE MUSIC" → "YOUR OWN MUSIC, YOUR OWN PLAYLISTS"** | The old heading reads as a compatibility badge. The objection in a stranger's head is "do I have to use some other library?", and the new heading answers it in the heading. |

It is the single most radio-like thing about the product and nothing else in the category does it. | **ON YOUR HOME SCREEN is new** | Widgets shipped in **1.4.0** and the description has never mentioned them — three releases of selling an eight-mode app while the entire Home Screen half went unsold. 1.4.2 and 1.4.3 are both widget releases. |

## 2. Keywords — one word, and it fits exactly

Live (93 of 100):

```
road,trip,visualizer,equalizer,mood,night,cassette,vinyl,car,radio,retro,aesthetic,visualiser
```

Paste this instead (**100 of 100**):

```
road,trip,visualizer,equalizer,mood,night,cassette,vinyl,car,radio,retro,aesthetic,visualiser,widget
```

**Why `widget` and why it is the best 7 characters available.** The app has
shipped two consecutive widget releases and the word appears in **no indexed
field** — not the name, not the subtitle, not the keywords. It is a term people
genuinely search on its own ("widgets", "music widget", "aesthetic widget"),
and because Apple combines the keyword field with the name and subtitle, one
word here also forms "music widget" with the subtitle's *music*. Apple matches
plurals, so the singular covers both.

It costs nothing: 93 + `widget` (6) + its comma (1) = exactly 100. The 7 spare
characters recorded on 15.09 as "deliberately left spare, because a weak word
costs a slot" were left for a word worth having, and this is one.

**The two standing rules still hold** and nothing here breaks them: no word is
repeated from the name (*cruise, fm, driving, visuals*) or the subtitle
(*music, drive, feels*), and there are no phrases — Apple splits on commas and
spaces and forms the combinations itself.

## 3. Subtitle — the real ASO decision, and it is the owner's

> **CORRECTED 2026-10-08, AND EVERYTHING BELOW THIS LINE WAS WRITTEN AGAINST
> THE WRONG LINE.** The live subtitle is **`Visual Music Player`** (19 of 30),
> read off the owner's own App Information screenshot — the character counter
> says 11 remaining, which is exactly 30 − 19, so it is the saved value and not
> a misread. `Music for how a drive feels` has not been the subtitle for some
> time and nothing in this repo recorded it changing. The analysis below is
> kept as the record of what was reasoned on 01.10; read it as being about the
> line the store used to carry.
>
> **WHAT THE REAL LINE COSTS, and it is one word rather than the whole field:**
> Apple matches plurals, so the subtitle's **`Visual`** and the name's
> **`Visuals`** are ONE term indexed twice — i.e. the store's second-most-
> weighted field spends a third of itself on a word the highest-weighted field
> already owns, with **11 characters spare**. `music` is carried exactly as
> before, `player` is a genuinely new term, and what was dropped costs nothing
> by this section's own reading (stop words, a term nobody searches, and
> *drive*, which the name already carries as *Driving*). Replacing the single
> word `Visual` with any term not already indexed adds a search term for free
> and changes nothing else about the line. **Option C below is therefore no
> longer the only way to improve this field.**
>
> **THE KEYWORD FIELD IS CLEAN AGAINST THE NEW LINE** — `visualizer` and
> `visualiser` are different words rather than plurals of `visual`, so section
> 2's plan to spend the last 7 characters on `widget` still stands. But if
> `Visual` is ever swapped for a word the keyword field already carries
> (`retro`, `radio`, `aesthetic`, `cassette`, `vinyl`), that keyword must come
> OUT of the field in the same edit.

Live until some point before 2026-10-08, and the line the rest of this section
is about (27 of 30):

```
Music for how a drive feels
```

**It is the best-written line on the page and the weakest-performing field.**
The subtitle is the second-highest-weighted thing in App Store search, and of
its 27 characters, *for*, *how* and *a* are stop words, *feels* is not a term
anybody searches, and *drive* is a near-duplicate of the name's *Driving*. In
practice it is spending the store's second-best field on the word **music**.

Three honest options, with what each costs:

**A — leave it alone.** It reads beautifully and it is the line the product's
voice is built on. Cost: the second-strongest field keeps carrying one useful
word.

**B — keep the voice, add one term** (30 of 30):

```
Music visuals for how a drive feels
```
— no, that is 35. The honest version of B does not exist: there is no room to
add a real search term without cutting words that make the line scan. Skip B.

**C — spend it on search** (30 of 30):

```
Music widgets & turntable deck
```

That adds **widgets**, **turntable** and **deck** to the index, keeps
**music**, and drops only *drive*, which the name already carries as *Driving*.
It reads as a list rather than a line of copy, which is the cost, and it is a
legitimate subtitle — Apple refuses keyword *stuffing*, not three nouns that
describe the app.

**Recommendation: C, and only if she is comfortable losing the line.** It is
worth more than everything else in this document combined, because it is a
weighted field rather than a conversion tweak. If the line stays, say so
plainly and stop calling the subtitle an ASO opportunity — it becomes a brand
decision, which is a perfectly good reason.

**If the subtitle ever does change, the keyword field must be re-read in the
same sitting**, because *widget* and *turntable* would then be repeats and
`widget` should come back out of the keywords to make room for something else.

### The one thing that cannot be done from here

**The widget screenshot has to be taken on her phone.** Widgets are Swift;
they cannot be compiled, rendered or photographed in this environment, and
there is no device here. What is needed is one screenshot of a Home Screen page
carrying Cruise FM tiles.

**It is the same job she already has to do for build 72.** The build-25 rule
says a build that changes native code must be launched on a phone and a large
Mode tile actually placed before anything goes to Apple. So:

1. Install the newest **TestFlight** build (71 — *not* 72, see below), open it
   once.
2. Press and hold the Home Screen → **+** → Cruise FM. Add a **large The Mode**
   tile, long-press it → **Edit Widget** → set the look to **Record** (that is
   the turntable, and the newest work in this build).
3. Add a **large Last Played** tile and set its look to **CD player** (the
   Winamp-style window).
4. If a Cruise FM tile is already on the Home Screen, **remove it and add it
   back** — iOS caches a widget's last successful draw per placed tile, so an
   unchanged-looking tile after an update is the cache rather than the work.
5. Screenshot that page and send it.

### Then, here — the exact mechanism

The slide set is an explicit list in `scripts/marketing/build-slides.mjs`
(`PHONE_SLIDES`), one row per slide: the source file, the surround tint, the
layout, and a two-line headline.

1. Her screenshot goes into `screenshots-appstore/` as **`11-widgets-home.jpg`**
   (the builder reads `.jpg` from that folder and base64-inlines it).
2. Add this row second in `PHONE_SLIDES`, and **delete the
   `08-yourphoto-framing` row**:

   ```js
   { f: '11-widgets-home', t: '<from tints.py>', s: 'A',
     a: 'Your moods,', b: 'on your Home Screen' },
   ```

   **Layout `A`, not `B`.** `B` runs the picture full-bleed with the headline
   laid over it, which on a Home Screen shot would put type across her app
   icons; `A` floats the phone in a tinted glow, which is what the other eight
   slides do.
3. `python3 scripts/marketing/tints.py screenshots-appstore` for the tint, then
   `node scripts/marketing/build-slides.mjs`.

**Re-running `tints.py` is not optional.** The surround belongs to the picture
it frames: the dial slide's surround went teal → amber when that screenshot was
re-taken, and the Night Run slide spent three weeks framed in a blue the app had
stopped using because nobody re-sampled it after the palette moved.

**The iPad set (8 slides) is a separate job** and a lower priority — there is no
widget slide there either, but it needs an iPad Home Screen screenshot, and the
iPad is the one device where the app has dropped the driving frame entirely.
Leave it unless she wants it.

## 7. The field nobody has used: the subscription names

**In-app purchase display names are indexed by App Store search.** The two
products are currently called **Premium Monthly** and **Premium Annual** — six
indexed words spent entirely on billing periods.

A display name is 30 characters, is shown to the customer on the paywall and on
their receipt, and changing it puts the product through review again (quick,
and it does not hold up the app). So it must stay honest and readable, which
rules out stuffing.

Modest, honest candidates:

```
Premium — Driving Visuals     (25)
Premium Visuals & Widgets     (25)
```

**Worth doing, but last.** The gain is small and uncertain, the names are
customer-facing, and there is no reason to touch anything in App Store Connect
during a review. Park it until 1.4.3 is approved.
